1 Risk Management — the never-ending cycle feeds everything else
Who's in the room: Senior management (owns risk), risk manager / CISO (runs the process), asset & system owners (provide data), IT ops, legal & compliance. Senior management is ultimately accountable — favorite exam answer.

Activities — in order

  • Identify assets & assign value ( AV ) → identify threats → identify vulnerabilities
  • Assess: likelihood (ARO) × impact (EF) — quantitatively or qualitatively (Heat map: Low/Med/High)
  • Calculate: SLE = AV × EF, then ALE = SLE × ARO
  • Treat — pick one of 4: Mitigate (reduce), Transfer (insure), Avoid (stop the activity), Accept (cost of control > ALE)
  • Control analysis + safeguard justification: (ALEbefore − ALEafter) − annual cost of safeguard
  • Monitor residual risk continuously → back to identify

FinCo does this here

Asset: customer-portal server, AV = $200,000. Threat: ransomware. Vulnerability: no offline backups, flat network. EF = 60% (60% would be lost/rewritten). SLE = 200k × 0.6 = $120,000. ARO = 0.5 (expected once per 2 years). ALE = 120k × 0.5 = $60,000. A DR solution costing $25k/yr that cuts EF to 10%: new ALE = 20k×0.5 = $10k → justification = 60k − 10k − 25k = +$25k/yr → BUY IT. (This is why FinCo later has a DR site at all.)

Exam traps

  • Safeguard value is the ALE before − ALE after − annual cost. If positive → implement.
  • Risk acceptance must be a conscious, senior-management decision — never default.
  • Total risk = Threat × Vuln × Asset; Residual = Total − controls.
  • Quantitative needs real $ numbers; qualitative (Delphi, heat maps) when numbers aren't available.
IN: asset inventory, threat intel, vulnerabilitiesOUT: risk register, ALE figures, decision to mitigate (→ drives BCP)
2 BCP Phase 1 — Project Scope & Planning who runs the show
Who's in the room: Senior management champion (without them the plan fails — #1 exam point), BCP committee (each critical business unit represented), CISO/DRP lead, legal, HR, IT, facilities.

Activities

  • Get senior management support & funding — they provide legitimacy & money
  • Define scope: whole org vs one business unit; and what planning types are in/out (BCP, DRP, IRP, …)
  • Perform business unit & process prioritization — decide who gets analyzed first
  • Agree on BCP team structure and roles; assign resources
  • Legal & regulatory requirements review (e.g., financial sector uptime duties)

FinCo does this here

CEO signs the charter; committee = rep from Retail Banking, Trading, IT, HR, Facilities, Legal. Scope = the whole firm, but Phase-1 analysis prioritizes Trading & Customer Portal first, corporate marketing last. Ransomware classed as the primary disaster scenario to plan for.

Exam traps

  • Senior management support = the single most critical element of BCP (appears constantly).
  • BCP committee must include representatives of every critical business unit — not just IT.
  • BCP scope: "all systems" is wrong answer framing — scope starts with most critical business processes.
IN: risk register, management mandateOUT: BCP charter, scoped priorities, committee roster
3 BCP Phase 2 — Business Impact Analysis (BIA) the heart of BCP
Who's in the room: BCP committee + business unit managers (they know their processes), risk manager, finance (to quantify losses). Business units, not IT, define criticality.

Activities — BIA sub-phases, in order

  • Identify priorities — rank business processes by criticality (asset value & process importance)
  • Risk identification — natural + man-made threats per process
  • Likelihood assessment — ARO per threat (often qualitative + quantitative mix)
  • Impact analysis — for each process define: MTD/MAO, RTO, WRT, RPO; quantify loss of: revenue, payroll, reputation, legal exposure, SLA credits
  • Resource prioritization — allocate people/$ to the processes with the smallest MTD first

The downtime ladder (memorize)

(FinCo: 4 days)

RTO + WRT ≤ MTD. And separately on the data axis: RPO = max tolerable data loss (how far back your last good backup is). FinCo's trading data: RPO ≈ 0 → needs real-time replication, not nightly tapes.

FinCo does this here

Committee finds: Trading platform MTD = 1 hour, portal MTD = 5 days, payroll MTD = 1 week. Email (still up during the attack) = MTD 3 days, low priority. Losses modeled per day of outage: trading $2M/day, portal $300k/day + reputational damage with regulators. This ranking later decides who gets restored first in the DRP.

Exam traps

  • BIA identifies critical business processes first — risk analysis identifies assets. (Classic A/B distractor.)
  • Maximum Tolerable Downtime (MAO) is decided in the BIA — then the DR solution is built to fit under it, never the reverse.
  • RPO is about data loss; RTO is about time to restore. WRT sits inside MTD after RTO.
IN: process inventory, process owners' inputOUT: MTD/RTO/WRT/RPO per process, recovery priority list, loss estimates
4 BCP Phase 3 — Continuity Planning design how to stay up
Who's in the room: BCP committee + operations managers; actual plan writing often delegated to the strategy development team, then approved by senior management.

Activities

  • Strategy development — pick provision approach per MTD (from BIA): which business units need which continuity provisions
  • Provisions & processes: people (alternate staff, cross-training), buildings/facilities, infrastructure (redundant systems, comms)
  • Backup strategy: full/incremental/differential; 3-2-1 rule; offsite storage; electronic vaulting / remote journaling / remote mirroring (RPO decides which!)
  • Mutual aid agreements & subscriptions; insurance review

FinCo decides: hot site with mirrored data for Trading (RPO≈0), warm site + nightly electronic vaulting for the Portal, cold site + weekly offsite tapes for payroll archiving. Backup = daily incremental + weekly full, copies offline & immutable (lesson from ransomware: online backups get encrypted too).

Exam traps

  • Site choice is driven by MTD from the BIA — cheapest site that fits under MTD wins.
  • Warm vs cold: warm has hardware pre-installed; cold is just the shell.
  • Remote journaling (hours of data) vs electronic vaulting (batch, offsite) vs mirroring (near-zero RPO).
  • Mutual aid agreements sound nice but fail operationally — know the caveat.
IN: MTD/RTO/RPO from BIA, budgetOUT: chosen strategies (sites, backups, alternates), continuity procedures
5 BCP Phase 4 — Approval & Implementation make it real
Who's in the room: Senior management (final approval — always), then everyone: the plan is distributed to all staff in relevant parts; training assigned.

Activities

  • Senior management approves the plan (legitimacy + accountability)
  • Implementation: provision the sites, buy the subscriptions, configure the backups
  • Training & education for recovery teams and staff
  • BCP = living document: version control, distribution list, review schedule (annual minimum)
FinCo: board approves; DR site contracted; immutable backup vault stands up; recovery team members named with alternates; everyone gets awareness training, recovery teams get role-specific drills.

Exam traps

  • Final plan approval = senior management (not the committee, not the CISO).
  • A BCP that isn't tested is a shelf document — testing (stage 7) is not optional.
IN: drafted plan from Phase 3OUT: approved, funded, trained, operational BCP
6 DRP — the day the plan fires FinCo, Saturday 3 AM
Who's in the room: DRP response team (named members + alternates from Phase 4), incident commander, senior management (activation & disaster declaration), comms/PR, legal. Note the split: BCP keeps the business running (strategic) · DRP restores operations after disruption (tactical).

Activities — in sequence

  • Detection & assessment: what happened, what's affected — FinCo: ransomware note found, 40 servers encrypted, portal down
  • Activation criteria check: does this meet the bar to declare a disaster? (Only senior mgmt / DRP director declares)
  • Containment first: isolate infected segments — do NOT restore into a still-infected network
  • Notify: insurer (ransomware = transfer risk), regulator (financial sector), law enforcement, customers per SLA
  • Recovery in BIA priority order: Trading platform → hot site (mirrored) up by 9 AM; Portal → warm site restore from last night's vault; payroll waits
  • Validate data integrity (WRT) before telling users "all clear"
  • Return to primary site: only after rebuilt & verified — then the reverse test
  • Lessons learned report → feeds plan updates (loop back to stage 1–2)
FinCo, hour by hour: 3:00 detection → 3:20 SOC isolates segments → 4:00 CEO declares disaster (activation criteria met) → 4:30 insurer & regulator notified → 6:00 hot site takes trading load (RTO 1 hr met — barely) → Sat evening portal restores from vault at warm site → Sun integrity checks (WRT) → Mon users back; data loss: last replication ≈ 15 min (RPO held for trading; portal lost 6 hrs of uploads vs its RPO... acceptable per MTD).

Exam traps

  • Declaration of disaster = senior management, based on predefined activation criteria.
  • First DRP step after assessment is contain the damage — restoring before containment re-infects everything.
  • Recovery order = BIA criticality order, never "most important server" by opinion.
  • Personnel safety is always the first priority in any disaster — before systems, before data.
IN: approved BCP/DRP, activation criteria, BIA prioritiesOUT: restored operations, lessons-learned report
7 Testing & Maintenance — prove it works escalating disruption
Who's in the room: DRP/BCP teams + auditors observe; management decides how much disruption the business can absorb for the test.

The 5 test types — least to most disruptive (memorize the order)

#TestWhat happensDisruption
1Checklist / read-throughcopies of plan distributed, staff read & confirmnone
2Structured walk-throughteam "walks" the scenario verbally, role-plays responsesnone
3Simulationrespond to a practice scenario, some real resources usedlow
4Parallelrecovery site actually activated, runs alongside productionlow–med
5Full interruptionprimary shut down for real, recover on the DR sitehigh — riskiest
FinCo: quarterly walk-throughs; annual parallel test of the hot site (trading runs 2 hrs from DR during lunch); full-interruption test scheduled only after two clean parallel tests — and with rollback criteria written down.

Exam traps

  • Know the exact order <b>checklist → walk-through → simulation → parallel → full-interruption</b> and that full-interruption is rarely used (business risk).
  • Tests should be unannounced to some degree for realism; results feed plan maintenance.
IN: approved plan, test scheduleOUT: test reports, plan updates (→ back to stage 1)
＋ PIA — Privacy Impact Assessment parallel track, personal data
Who's in the room: Data/process owner, privacy officer / DPO, legal & compliance, security engineer; results go to senior management.

Activities

  • Triggered whenever a system/change handles personally identifiable information (PII) — new system, new data use, new vendor, new cross-border flow
  • Inventory the data: what PII, why collected, lawful basis, where stored, who accesses it, retention
  • Identify privacy risks: over-collection, secondary use, unauthorized access, disclosure, no deletion path
  • Assess & mitigate: minimization, consent, encryption/pseudonymization, access controls, retention limits
  • Document & review — PIA revisited on significant change (privacy by design: PIA is done early in SDLC, not after launch)
FinCo tie-in: when the portal is rebuilt at the DR site, the rebuilt environment re-uses customer PII → a PIA confirms the DR copies get the same encryption, retention and deletion rules as production — and that backups restored at the warm site don't leave PII exposed to a wider admin group than in prod.

Exam traps

  • PIA focuses on privacy impact to individuals — BIA focuses on impact to business processes. Don't swap them.
  • PIA is performed before/while implementing a system handling PII (privacy by design), and updated with changes.
IN: data inventory, system designs, privacy regs (GDPR/CCPA)OUT: PIA report, mitigation decisions, sign-off by privacy officer

The one-line mental model

  • Risk analysis finds what could hurt the business and prices it (ALE).
  • BIA finds how long each process can be down (MTD) and how much data may be lost (RPO).
  • BCP is the strategy to keep running; DRP is the playbook for the worst day; testing proves both; PIA guards the customers' data through all of it.
  • Exam default answer: senior management approves/supports/is accountable — at every stage.
