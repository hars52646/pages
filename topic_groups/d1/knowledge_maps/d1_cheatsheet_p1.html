Adhere and be seen to...The ISC2 Code of Ethics preamble establishes that the safety and welfare of society and the common good, the duty to our principals, and to each other, requires that we adhere — and be seen to adhere — to the highest ethical standards. Strict adherence is a condition of certification: violating the code can cost the credential itself.
First canon: protect whatCanon 1 (highest priority): protect society, the common good, necessary public trust and confidence, and the infrastructure. Key phrase to memorize: 'Protect society…'. It supersedes all other canons — including service to employers — whenever conflicts arise. [Q538]
Second canon: act howCanon 2: act honorably, honestly, justly, responsibly, and legally. Key phrase: 'Act honorably…'. Ranks second — below protecting society but above service to principals and above advancing the profession.
Third canon: serve whomCanon 3: provide diligent and competent service to principals (employers and clients). Key phrase: 'Diligent service…'. Ranks third: society and honorable, legal conduct outrank employer service.
Fourth canon: advance whatCanon 4: advance and protect the profession. Key phrase: 'Advance the profession…'. Lowest priority. Sharing threat intelligence and collaborating with peers across organizations primarily fulfills this canon. [Q559]
Lower number always winsCanon priority is absolute: a lower-numbered canon always supersedes a higher-numbered one — no exceptions. The four canons are applied in priority order. Ordering: society (1) > honorable/legal conduct (2) > principals/employer (3) > profession (4). [Q538]
Compliant but unethicalCompliance means meeting legal and regulatory minimums. Ethics goes beyond compliance — doing what is right even when not legally required. An action can be fully compliant yet still unethical. Exam distinction: compliance is the floor; ethics exceeds it.
Policies drive consistent...Organizational ethical behavior is instilled through policies that prescribe consistent conduct across all employees. For a multinational with varying cultural norms, codify ethics into formal corporate policies enforced by senior management. Letting each region set its own standards perpetuates inconsistency and undermines enterprise-wide governance. [Q398]

Exam framing: ethics questions are deceptively tricky scenarios where two canons seem to conflict; the reward goes to strict canon ordering applied under pressure.

Scenario: a SOC discovering an employee accessed payment records without business justification is a confidentiality violation — not authenticity, which verifies the origin/legitimacy of assets rather than whether access was authorized. [Q410]
Unauthorized modification...Integrity protects against unauthorized or accidental modification. Mechanisms: hashing, digital signatures, checksums, input validation. Integrity means maintaining data accuracy and trustworthiness by preventing unauthorized or unintended modifications, ensuring information remains reliable for business decisions throughout its lifecycle. 'Proof of origin' points...When a question mentions 'proof of origin' or verifying the sender, choose authenticity or nonrepudiation — not confidentiality. Sender-verification scenarios never resolve to confidentiality, which concerns preventing disclosure.
Enable business,...Security controls exist to enable the business and manage risk to an acceptable level — never to maximize one CIA property at the expense of mission or usability.
I-A-A-A sequenceThe access control chain: Identification (claim an identity — username, swipe card), Authentication (prove the claim — password, biometric, token), Authorization (determine what you can do — permissions, RBAC role assignment), Accountability (trace actions back to a subject — audit logs tied to unique IDs).

can you doAuthorization determines what an authenticated subject can do — permissions and RBAC (Role-Based Access Control) role assignment. It follows only after identity is verified.

Preventive + detective +...A 'complete' control at each layer combines preventive, detective, and corrective capabilities. Defense in depth requires preventive, detective, and corrective controls at every layer so attacks can be stopped, caught, and remediated regardless of where they occur. [Q778]
