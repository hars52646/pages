2 · The family tree (first split on the exam)

The discretionary / non-discretionary split decides half the answers before you read the options.

Discretionary someone with discretion decides

DAC — the data owner decides (grants/revokes via ACLs).
RuBAC — a variant of DAC: centrally administered rule-sets/ACLs, not labels.

Non-discretionary system policy decides

MAC — labels decide (clearance vs classification).
RBAC — roles decide.  ABAC — attributes + policies decide.

4 · Model cards

Facts to know, when to choose it, and what the exam writers will try to slip past you.

MAC

Non-discretionary↑ map

“System-enforced labels decide — users cannot touch them.”

Key facts

  • Access decided by system-enforced central policy; users cannot change labels.
  • Subjects carry clearance, objects carry classification; access only when clearance dominates classification (including categories/compartments).
  • Hierarchy: Unclassified → Confidential → Secret → Top Secret.
  • Accurate labeling is the non-negotiable foundation — enforcement is meaningless if labels are wrong.
  • Non-discretionary label enforcement = least privilege with strict information-flow control.

Choose it when

  • Government / intelligence agency enforcing strict classification levels and regulatory compliance.
  • PCI DSS cardholder-data protection via sensitivity labels and predefined clearance levels.
  • The scenario prizes confidentiality and flow control over flexibility.

Watch out

  • “Labels & clearance” in the stem → MAC, not RuBAC (RuBAC is DAC-family).
  • Hierarchical classification is only a component — the mechanism is non-discretionary labels.
  • MAC is label-driven, not “stricter RBAC”.

DAC

Discretionary↑ map

“The data owner decides — via ACLs they grant and revoke.”

Key facts

  • The data owner decides who gets access, via ACLs, permission bits, grants/revokes.
  • Windows: security descriptors hold a DACL of SIDs (fine-grained per user/group). Unix: rwx owner/group/other bits (coarser — three buckets).
  • Strength: business-driven flexibility for classification and lifecycle needs.
  • Core vulnerability: owners can over-grant → excessive permissions, privilege creep, inconsistent enforcement.
  • Enterprise failure mode: different owners apply different standards → gaps, over-permissioning, audit pain (why RBAC wins at scale).

Choose it when

  • Managers must directly control their team’s documents with granular grant/revoke over their own files.
  • The business demands owner-tailored flexibility.

Watch out

  • #1 DAC-vs-RBAC limitation: inconsistent policies across departments (least-privilege difficulty is secondary).
  • #1 DAC vulnerability: owners over-grant excessive or inappropriate permissions.
  • HIPAA threat pattern: doctors granting temporary access without oversight or documentation.
  • IT/sysadmins granting without owner consultation = non-discretionary anti-pattern; it breaks accountability & least privilege — that is the problem, not the fix.
  • Governance fixes: central policy guides owner discretion; automated auditing; clear ownership in cloud DAC.

RuBAC

Discretionary (DAC variant)↑ map

“Centrally-managed if/then rules decide — a variant of DAC.”

Key facts

  • A form of DAC that uses centrally managed ACLs — not a MAC label model.
  • Decisions are if/then conditional rules evaluated at access-request time: time-of-day, protocol/port, source IP, department, location, action type.
  • Rules live centrally → end users cannot alter or circumvent them (tamper-resistant).
  • Lives on the perimeter: firewalls, routers, gateways — e.g. a VPN gateway blocking outside business hours.
  • Fine-grained per-user / per-group granularity via individually mapped ACL entries (RBAC lacks per-user rights).

Choose it when

  • Access is gated by conditions (time, protocol, department) rather than identity or role alone.
  • EHR-style: restrict by protocol + time + department and users must not modify their own permissions → centrally defined ACLs (MAC fails — labels).
  • Consistent branch-office perimeter enforcement (retail routers with protocol/time rules).

Watch out

  • #1 trap: RuBAC is a form of DAC.
  • Labels & clearance → MAC. Conditions → RuBAC.
  • Time-gated VPN → RuBAC, not RBAC (roles grant by job, not by the clock).
  • Inherent limitation: every rule individually created and maintained → admin burden → misconfiguration risk.
  • Global scale: scalability + admin overhead dominate; fix = automated rule-conflict detection & policy optimization — never delegation to departments.
  • Compliance demands audit trails of rule changes AND access decisions.
  • Enterprise advantage = streamlined administration & scalability (not “automated unauthorized-access detection” — that is monitoring).

RBAC

Non-discretionary↑ map

“Roles decide — users inherit permissions only through roles.”

Key facts

  • Two mappings: user → role, role → permission; users never receive permissions directly.
  • Change the role once, every member updates — administratively scalable.
  • NIST variants: Core/flat; Hierarchical (inheritance — regional rolls into global); Constrained (SoD via mutually-exclusive roles); Symmetric (+ permission review); Limited (cardinality/nesting caps, time windows preserving roles).
  • Static and identity-only: cannot detect behavioral anomalies (insider misuse, credential theft, session hijacking) → complement with SIEM, UEBA, PAM.
  • Metrics: role-burst count, permission-to-role ratio, role-reassignment latency, recertification compliance, orphaned accounts.

Choose it when

  • 50,000 employees / 20 countries / frequent reorgs → RBAC (ABAC complexity impractical at this scale).
  • Rapid cloud scaling, multi-jurisdiction → hierarchical RBAC.
  • Least privilege + SoD → granular job-function roles; mutually-exclusive roles against fraud (Payment-Initiator ≠ Payment-Approver).
  • Time-zone restrictions while preserving trader roles → Limited RBAC (ABAC = unnecessary complexity).
  • GLBA/GDPR → roles limiting access by job function + location.
  • DevOps CI/CD → dynamic RBAC with automated role assignment.

Watch out

  • Role drift in fast-changing orgs → automated recertification, role mining, manager attestation.
  • No per-user granularity — that belongs to RuBAC’s mapped ACL entries.
  • Layering: RBAC = structural base, ABAC adds per-request conditions (time, location, device, risk).
  • ST&E of an RBAC system → targeted penetration testing, not just configuration compliance checks.

ABAC

Non-discretionary↑ map

“Attributes decide — subject + object + environment, evaluated in real time.”

Key facts

  • Evaluates subject attrs (role, clearance, dept, citizenship, training), object attrs (classification, data type, owner, sensitivity tags), environment attrs (time, location, device posture, threat level, network) against Boolean policies in real time.
  • Most expressive of the core models; no predefined role constraints.
  • Pipeline: attributes synced from authoritative sources (HR, IdP, asset DB) → PDP evaluates → PEPs (API gateways, proxies, middleware) enforce. Stale attributes = wrong decisions.
  • IAM integration key: attribute synchronization + policy rule management — before evaluation accuracy matters.

Choose it when

  • Dynamic gating on time + location + threat level + data sensitivity (multinational financial).
  • Zero Trust: continuous authentication + real-time policy evaluation.
  • Hybrid cloud, diverse infrastructure & compliance (GDPR/CCPA multi-region); GDPR data minimization.
  • IoT / smart city: real-time device attributes at scale.
  • DevOps CI/CD least privilege + SoD where static RBAC cannot keep pace.

Watch out

  • Primary obstacle = complexity of defining & managing context rules — NOT performance (secondary).
  • Finer granularity → harder-to-manage rule sets; misconfiguration risk is the governance concern.
  • AI integration is dual-use: dynamic adaptation and biased decision-making — correct answers name both.
  • At massive stable-org scale, RBAC wins — ABAC maintenance overhead is the reason.

6 · Trap strip

The eleven discriminating facts that flip the most answers.

  1. RuBAC is a form of DAC. Centrally-administered rule variant — never a MAC sibling.
  2. Labels & clearance → MAC; conditions (time/protocol/dept) → RuBAC.
  3. Time-gated VPN → RuBAC, not RBAC. Role shift-assignment grants by role, not by the clock.
  4. DAC vs RBAC #1 flaw: inconsistent policies across departments (least-privilege difficulty is secondary).
  5. DAC #1 vulnerability: owners over-grant → excessive permissions & privilege creep.
  6. RBAC is static & identity-only → pair with SIEM/UEBA/PAM; layer ABAC for per-request conditions.
  7. Scale → RBAC; dynamic context → ABAC. 50k users + ABAC = impractical complexity.
  8. ABAC’s obstacle = rule complexity & manageability — performance is only secondary.
  9. ABAC + AI is dual-use: dynamic adaptation AND biased decisions — right answers name both.
  10. Windows = DACL + SIDs (fine-grained); Unix = rwx bits (coarse, three buckets).
  11. Sysadmin grants without owner consultation = non-discretionary anti-pattern → broken accountability & least privilege.
