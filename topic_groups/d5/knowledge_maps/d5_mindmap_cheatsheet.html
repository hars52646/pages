Right subjects, protected...Access Control Foundations define how organizations ensure that only the right subjects can interact with protected objects — the bedrock of Domain 5 and the conceptual gateway to every authentication, authorization, and accountability mechanism tested on the CISSP.
IAAA sequence orderThe four IAAA services occur in strict sequence: Identification, then Authentication, then Authorization, then Accountability. Steps cannot be skipped: authorization without authentication is meaningless, and accountability without unique identifiers is impossible.
Claim without proofIdentification is the act of claiming an identity to a system, such as presenting a username, badge, or card swipe. It is a public assertion only and provides no inherent security because it involves no verification — it is a label, not a control. [Q159]
Zero security by itselfWhen identification is described as a badge or username claim, remember it provides zero security by itself — treat identification as an assertion with no protective value on its own, because it merely claims identity without any verification step. [Q159]
First link to digital...Unique identifier assignment is the foundational step of Identification, enabling accountability by linking each subject to a distinct digital identity upon which authentication and authorization depend. [Q91]
Validates the claimAuthentication proves the claimed identity (for example with a password or biometric) and is the second IAAA step. It validates the identification claim and acts as one supporting component of accountability rather than an end in itself. [Q121]
can you doAuthorization grants rights and privileges to an authenticated subject and determines what that subject can do. It occurs only after authentication, because granting rights to an unverified identity is meaningless. [Q166]
did what, provableAccountability ensures user actions are logged and monitored, enabling auditors to trace activities to specific users and preventing denial of actions (non-repudiation). It is the service that links actions to identities for audit and for enforcing consequences. [Q155 Q164]
Unique ID AND loggingAccountability requires both unique user identifiers and logging — neither alone is sufficient. Logging without unique IDs (such as shared accounts) breaks accountability entirely, creating traceability gaps and making non-repudiation impossible. This is the foundation when a question asks about the foundation of accountability. [Q122]
Four componentsAccountability requires four components working together: unique identification, authentication, authorization, and logging. [Q125]
Linchpin of access controlAccountability is the foundational principle underpinning the entire access control system because it ensures actions are traceable and users cannot deny their activities. It is the linchpin of security governance: without unique identification and logging, no policy can be enforced, audited, or improved — always ask who did what, and can you prove it. [Q158]
Consequences by designWhen a security architect must ensure users face consequences for misuse through the overall access control design, the correct answer is accountability — not availability, which only ensures system uptime and does not address tracing actions or enforcing user responsibility. [Q127]

Match scenario keywords (facility, server room, building) to physical; (system, data, application) to logical. [Q106]
CRUD plus executeLogical access control systems manage file operation modes such as create, read, update, delete (CRUD), plus read/write and execute permissions — these modes define the granularity of object interaction. [Q110]

Prove the claimed identityAuthentication is how a subject proves a claimed identity — the second step in the IAAA chain (Identification, then Authentication, then Authorization, then Accountability). CISSP requires distinguishing factor types, recognizing possession-factor implementations across scenarios (banking, IoT, enterprise), and applying managerial judgment on security-versus-usability trade-offs.
Administrative backboneIdentity proofing and the provisioning lifecycle form the administrative backbone of IAM. The exam tests reasoning about when access is created, changed, and removed, and what evidence supports a claimed identity before any account exists. Lifecycle control is a managerial, risk-reduction process rewarding timely revocation, periodic recertification, and automation that eliminates orphan accounts over technical elegance.
