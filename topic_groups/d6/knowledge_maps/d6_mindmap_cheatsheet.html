1 · Strategy & Parties

assess · test · audit▦ – flashcards↑ map

“Verify, do not redesign — scope first, risk-based always, independence decides credibility.”

AspectFacts
DefinitionsAssessment = holistic, judgment-based evaluation · test = authorized hands-on technical validation · audit = evidence examined against criteria, may attest. None redesigns controls.
Party ladder1st = self (lowest credibility) · 2nd = customer/counterparty (external but not independent) · 3rd = independent provider (highest). Internal audit is first-party org-wise but must stay independent of IT operations.
PlanningObjectives and scope first (Q6) — before teams, cadence, interviews, schedules. Allocate by business impact, likelihood, change rate, exposure, legal duty — not asset count. Under global time and resource constraints the critical overview element is overall scope and objectives — the timeline is secondary (Q61).
CadenceChange velocity drives frequency: CI/CD, agile, auto-scaling → continuous validation (Q53). Deployment rate, not the calendar, decides.
DeliveryRemote = scalable, repeatable, weak on physical · on-site = irreplaceable for physical controls, paper, air-gapped (Q129) · hybrid = tier method and frequency by location risk (Q134, Q80).
TeamsCapability = diverse expertise, qualifications, independence, clear roles, secure collaboration, documented method (Q76, Q77). Six quality gates: independence, skill, methodology, authorization, safety, actionable reporting.
GlobalCommon-core framework + local modules (ISO 27001 / COBIT-style flexibility) (Q62, Q66). Hybrid audits inspect end-to-end flows and cross-platform IAM integration (Q9, Q10, Q108).
CISSP lensPick the lawful, risk-based, independent, actionable option; safety, continuity and legality before technical completeness or realism.

Cues that point here

  • “MOST important first step” → define objectives & scope (Q6)
  • Fast-changing technology → rate of change sets cadence (Q53)
  • Physical controls / air-gapped / paper → on-site (Q129); the key remote-vs-on-site factor is comprehensive coverage of both physical and logical controls (Q64)
  • Most credible assurance → independent third party
  • Unequal risk across offices → tier sites and cadence (Q134)

Traps

  • Outsourcing ≠ prioritization — risk ranking stays managerial (Q94).
  • Uniform coverage ≠ comprehensive — risk-adjust depth and frequency (Q93).
  • External ≠ independent — a second party has a business interest.
  • Off-peak destructive testing still risks disruption — RoE define limits (Q73).

2 · Privacy & PIA

cross-border data▦ – flashcards↑ map

“Map every flow, pick the most restrictive law on the exam, and never copy production PII for realism.”

AspectFacts
TriggersPIA for a new system, new data collection, new use of existing data, changed processing scope, or a new cross-border transfer. DPIA = GDPR high-risk processing.
SequenceIdentify need → map data flows → assess privacy risk → define mitigation → approval / sign-off → residual risk to the risk register.
Cross-borderMap origin, transit path, storage location, access points, responsible actors, applicable laws (Q119). Exam picks the most restrictive jurisdiction (GDPR floor); reality maps every regime + counsel.
TransfersAdequacy decision (simplest) → SCCs (external transfers) → BCRs (intra-group recurring) → Article 49 derogations (exceptional, never the default).
RegimesGDPR opt-in orientation vs California CCPA/CPRA rights: know, delete, correct, opt out of sale/sharing, limit sensitive data.
Test dataSynthetic, anonymized or irreversibly masked preferred; compliance outranks realism (Q128). Pseudonymization is reversible — still controlled by residual risk.
Global PIAUmbrella assessment + jurisdiction annotations for consistent governance; localized assessments when national law, culture or privacy regimes genuinely differ (Q2).

Cues that point here

  • New cross-border HR cloud → localized PIAs per country (Q2)
  • Choosing remote vs on-site for global analytics → decided by mapping flows and gaps, not cost (Q119)
  • Recurring intra-group transfer → BCRs; no adequacy → SCCs
  • High-risk GDPR processing → DPIA, not generic PIA

Traps

  • Pseudonymized test data ≠ anonymized (Q128).
  • Article 49 derogations are exceptional — not a routine transfer route.
  • Exam simplifies to one best answer; real conflicts need counsel and every-regime mapping.

3 · Vulnerability Management & SCAP

scan · CVE/CVSS▦ – flashcards↑ map

“Scanning finds known weakness at breadth; SCAP is the language, CVSS the severity — neither is risk.”

AspectFacts
ScanningBreadth at scale: CVEs, open ports, versions, missing patches, misconfigurations — identification, never exploitation (Q46, Q113). Credentialed scans log in for local software/patch/config depth and fewer FNs; unauthenticated observe banners from outside — shallow (Q38, Q39).
ErrorsFP = reported but not real (wasted effort); FN = real but unreported — hidden risk and false confidence; FN is the dangerous one (Q19, Q25). Zero findings on an unauthenticated scan ≠ secure.
SCAPSecurity Content Automation Protocol — standardized machine-readable vulnerability/configuration content for cross-vendor exchange. A language, not a scanner (Q24, Q41); vs BAS: SCAP standardizes content, BAS actively simulates (Q130). Components: XCCDF checklists/benchmarks (Q28) · OVAL system-state tests · CVE vulnerability identifiers · CPE platform names · CCE configuration IDs.
CVSS v3.10.0 None · 0.1–3.9 Low · 4.0–6.9 Medium · 7.0–8.9 High · 9.0–10.0 Critical (7.0 starts High, 9.0 starts Critical). Severity input — not the business priority (Q37).
PrioritizationContextual risk formula: Risk = Threat × Vulnerability × Asset Value (6.3 §2). Use CVSS as an input, then add exposure, data sensitivity, asset criticality and known exploitation (CISA KEV). Exposure, compensating controls and business impact reorder remediation — a medium on an exposed critical asset can outrank a critical on an isolated one. SCAP + BAS proves demonstrated exploitability (Q34, Q70, Q89, Q124).
Humans & AIComplex business logic, workflow abuse, algorithm errors, nuanced authorization bypass need human review (Q81). AI accelerates initial discovery and correlation; humans validate and contextualize (Q57).

Cues that point here

  • Thousands of endpoints → automated vulnerability scanning (Q46, Q113)
  • Six incompatible scanners → adopt SCAP-compliant formats (Q24)
  • Write a hardening benchmark → XCCDF (Q28)
  • Score a vulnerability’s severity → CVSS (Q37)
  • Trading-algorithm logic errors → manual review (Q81)
  • Complex multi-tier app → automated scanning + targeted manual testing of critical components (Q106)

Traps

  • SCAP standardizes content — it is not a scanner (Q24).
  • Known signatures miss zero-days → add behavioral BAS + threat intel (Q63).
  • Raw counts and scores mislead — contextual risk decides (Q117).

4 · Penetration Testing & Adversarial Exercises

exploit · red/blue/purple · BAS▦ – flashcards↑ map

“Exploit within authorized scope; test the organization, not just the patch list; safety and legality gate everything.”

AspectFacts
ProcessReconnaissance → scanning/enumeration → vulnerability analysis → exploitation → post-exploitation → reporting — memorize the order.
Recon vs enumRecon = passive information gathering (OSINT, job postings, DNS — destcert). Enumeration = active probing of ports/services. Banner grabbing collects service strings and usually counts as active; fingerprinting infers OS/app from packet and system behavior even when banners lie (Q30, Q42).
Knowledge vs awarenessBlack / grey / white box = tester knowledge (none / partial / full). Blind / double-blind = defender awareness — double-blind evaluates detection and response under surprise.
Assumed breachStarts from an internal foothold or valid access → tests segmentation and lateral-movement resistance; provides little perimeter-defense evidence (Q69).
GovernanceSigned written authorization (scope, timing, RoE, exclusions) precedes testing — certifications and verbal approval never substitute (Q5). Production breach simulations need explicit stakeholder consent (Q133). Unclear scope → key systems untested (Q102). RoE — not off-hours timing — define prohibited actions (Q73).
TeamsRed = realistic adversary across people/process/technology (Q82) · Blue = detect, respond, harden · Purple = continuous Red↔Blue collaboration process (not a permanent squad); differentiator = rapid knowledge transfer (Q75); success = persistent detection/response improvement; a coordinated purple exercise tests control effectiveness and incident-response readiness together — passive monitoring does not exercise the human response function (Q4); threat intel makes scenarios plausible (Q65); SDLC purple improves controls pre-deployment (Q51); the strategic challenge is harmonizing offensive tactics with defensive priorities inside risk assessment (Q72); logic-bomb threats → purple exercises build proactive detection and IR readiness (Q109).
BASBreach and Attack Simulation — safely, continuously emulates attacker TTPs to validate control detection/prevention (Q21, Q43). Complements, never replaces, periodic human testing; never assigns CVE IDs.
SafetyHealthcare, utilities, water, power: simulate first + tightly limited live validation; availability and public safety outrank realism (Q52, Q86, Q100). Sustained testing of critical infrastructure yields public/stakeholder trust (Q54). Against AI-enhanced attacks: adaptive learning posture — a tool alone is a tactic, not the strategy (Q95).

Cues that point here

  • Defenders must stay unaware → double-blind
  • Internal segmentation / lateral movement → assumed breach
  • Resilience across people, process, tech → red team (Q82)
  • Ongoing automated attack validation → BAS (Q21)
  • Continuous Red–Blue knowledge transfer → purple (Q75)

Traps

  • Assumed breach is blind to perimeter effectiveness (Q69).
  • Pen test skips execution in vulnerability assessments; VA jumps from identification to reporting (destcert).
  • Off-peak destructive tests still disrupt — RoE + non-destructive methods (Q73).
  • BAS does not assign CVE identifiers.

5 · Software Testing & Threat Modeling

SAST · DAST · STRIDE▦ – flashcards↑ map

“Rerun the baseline after every change; shift left with SAST; model threats before code exists.”

AspectFacts
RegressionRerun established tests against a known-good baseline after ANY change — code, security patch, new feature, configuration, detection rules (Q33). Scope includes downstream dependents: shared libraries, APIs, data formats propagate breakage (Q40). Patches touch core paths (higher risk) but config changes silently alter firewall/GPO/routing/logging behavior — both get rigor (Q27).
OwnershipDevelopers and QA execute tests; management makes the risk-based go/no-go decision. Evidence = version-controlled baseline + repeatable reruns (Q26); change-management records prove workflow, not function.
AutomationFrequent releases / analyst-hours strain → automated regression suites in CI/CD preserving coverage — never cut cases or merely add headcount (Q36, Q44). CFO case = production, release and revenue risk (Q31).
SASTStatic analysis of source/bytecode/binary without execution — the commit-time shift-left choice before a functional app exists (Q79, Q111). Finds hardcoded secrets, injection patterns, insecure API use; noisier (more FPs).
DASTTests the running application outside-in, black-box, no source; finds XSS, auth bypass, runtime misconfiguration; needs a deployable instance — later and slower (Q111).
FuzzingInjects random / malformed / boundary input into a running executable → crashes, memory leaks, buffer overflows, unhandled exceptions (Q110, Q120). Mutation fuzzers are structure-blind; generation fuzzers understand input format (destcert).
Misuse & reviewMisuse cases model attacker/negative paths from threat models; confirm fail-securely: deny, log, no leakage, no insecure crash (Q118). Human code review finds logic flaws scanners miss; Fagan = formal six-step inspection (destcert).
Threat modelingEarliest SDLC risk technique (requirements/design), iterative — updated as architecture, suppliers and threats change; a single pre-coding list rapidly goes stale (Q67); includes vendor trust boundaries (Q138); a continuous program beats monthly pentests (Q116). Artifacts: DFDs, entry points, actors, assets, trust boundaries.
STRIDESpoofing→authentication · Tampering→integrity · Repudiation (denial of an action)→non-repudiation via accountability evidence · Information Disclosure→confidentiality · Denial of Service→availability · Elevation of Privilege→authorization.
Levels & blockchainTest levels ladder: unit → interface → integration → system (destcert); coverage efficiency via equivalence partitioning + boundary values. Deployed smart contracts may be immutable → formal verification, audit, testnet simulation before deployment (Q101).

Cues that point here

  • Login fails after a security patch → regression gap (Q35)
  • New feature works, stable reporting breaks → downstream regression (Q40)
  • Early non-functional project, limited resources → SAST (Q111)
  • Shift left → automated scans on each commit with immediate feedback — manual review only before release is a late bottleneck (Q88)
  • Unknown crashes / overflows → fuzzing (Q120)
  • Earliest SDLC risk technique → threat modeling (Q138)

Traps

  • Testing only new features, never rerunning prior cases → previously functional software breaks undetected (Q22); the baseline comparison is the proof (Q20).
  • Severe SIEM patch broke correlation → keep the patch, regression-test rules — no blind rollback (Q29).
  • Misuse testing ≠ ordinary functional testing.
  • DAST cannot run pre-build; SAST can (Q111).
  • Blockchain remediation barrier = immutability, beyond needing special scanners (Q101).

6 · Logging, Metrics & Reporting

SIEM · KPI/KRI▦ – flashcards↑ map

“Logs are evidence — preserve history, clip noise, sync clocks; metrics tell the board what changed.”

AspectFacts
Logs as evidenceSupport investigation and audit — preserve required history and integrity while tuning noise. Post-incident analysis reconstructs the timeline and scope (Q12) — forensic, not real-time alerting. Monitor for errors (404 floods), unauthorized modification (attacker patching behind a backdoor), breach indicators (mining, exfiltration, ransomware) (destcert).
Clipping levelThreshold suppressing routine below-threshold activity; surfaces anomalies; cuts noise, fatigue, storage, processing (Q11, Q13). Exam may phrase it as log-only-after-threshold; reality often keeps detail and clips alerts.
Circular loggingCap + overwrite oldest → bounded storage (Q14, Q16). Primary risk = silent loss of earliest forensic evidence; in delayed discovery the old entries are overwritten — missing, not corrupted (Q18).
RetentionDeciding factor for overwrite acceptability = whether historical evidence must be preserved (Q15); forensics/legal → sequential non-overwrite under policy. Tiers: hot (recent, fast) → warm (compliance age) → cold (long-term, legal hold) (Q56) — tiering plus variable retention and suitable clipping balances compliance, forensics and cost better than blanket deletion. Regulation, policy and legal holds overrule storage optimization.
CentralizationUnified SIEM across on-prem + cloud; NTP/PTP synchronized timestamps in one consistent format, or event sequencing becomes unreliable and cross-environment correlation breaks; separate tools fragment analysis into silos (Q55).
SIEM pipelineGeneration → transmission (realtime) → collection/aggregation → normalization → analysis (correlation, rules, statistics) → retention → disposal (secure, defensible) (destcert).
KPI vs KRIKPI = “are we doing it well?” (process: patch SLA, MTTR). KRI = “are we exposed?” (risk: exploitable criticals). Missed KPI → improve process; KRI breach → escalate to management. Trends beat snapshots; test counts are activity, multi-year time-to-patch is effectiveness (Q23).
Risk weightingCompare unequal programs by risk-adjusted impact, not counts (Q98); KRIs focus scarce testing — do not broaden blindly (Q97); dynamic KPIs adjust with threat intel + incident data (Q103); map one metric set to NIST CSF / ISO 27001 / GDPR (Q8); global mix of quantitative + qualitative KRIs (Q127); GDPR 72-hour notification metric when the rule applies.
SLA wording“Critical vulnerabilities within seven days” is a common benchmark, not a universal SLA — policy, active exploitation, asset criticality and compensating controls determine the actual deadline (6.2 §6).
ReportingRight audience, right language: board = business/financial impact, visuals, priorities, decisions (Q90, Q91, Q135) · engineers = assets, evidence, reproduction, remediation · auditors = methodology, traceability, control relevance · multi-level audiences get per-role tailoring incl. middle management; SOC 2 findings map to Trust Services Criteria (Q85). Every recommendation: owner + action + date; leadership needs prioritized, actionable recommendations rather than exhaustive technical specifications (Q125). Disclose scope limits and blind spots before trusting severity (Q47).
Output maturityRaw XML/CSV → normalized CVE + CVSS findings → detailed technical report → executive summary.

Cues that point here

  • Flood of isolated single failed logins → clipping thresholds + correlation (Q17)
  • Breach found weeks later, circular logs → overwritten history (Q18)
  • Improvement over years → time-to-patch trend (Q23)
  • Compare unequal divisions → risk-adjusted metrics
  • EHR dosage-calculation regression to the board → audience-relevant risk and impact metrics, not raw pass/fail (Q32)
  • Hybrid-cloud correlation → central SIEM + NTP (Q55)

Traps

  • Forwarding noisy events to the SIEM moves the problem — clip or correlate (Q17).
  • Circular-logging risk is evidence loss, not cost or corruption (Q16).
  • Standardized file formats ≠ comparative trend insight (Q58).
  • Annual KPI updates are not “dynamic” (Q103).

7 · Assurance, Emerging Tech & Resilience

SOC · ISO · DR ladder▦ – flashcards↑ map

“Match evidence to purpose; safety caps intrusiveness; only a restore test proves recovery.”

AspectFacts
SOC familySOC 1 = financial-reporting controls (SSAE 18 / ISAE 3402) · SOC 2 = Trust Services Criteria detail, restricted distribution — Type I design at a date, Type II operating effectiveness over 6–12 months (stronger; still an attestation, not a certification) · SOC 3 = public summary, no NDA, insufficient for gap analysis.
TSC ruleSecurity is always in scope; Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, Privacy depend on engagement scope.
FrameworksISO 27001 = ISMS certification; HITRUST = healthcare-prescriptive, harmonizes ISO/NIST/HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)/PCI. SOC 2 vs ISO decided by detailed tested controls vs internationally recognized management-system certification. Evidence strength: tests of controls + results beat objectives/descriptions (Q71); period evidence proves sustained operation; granular log-monitoring tests reveal SIEM gaps better than an overall opinion (Q83).
Third-party riskFavorable report ≠ guarantee — review scope, period, exceptions, CUECs, residual risk. Discrepancies: notify, gap analysis, critical fixes, communicate plan, verify closure, escalate (Q49). Global scale: tier vendors + SOC 2 Type II + continuous monitoring (Q50). Expand scope for AI and IoT risk (Q60). Sustained Type II monitoring should visibly improve practices and awareness — documentation without behavioral change is checkbox compliance (Q99).
AuditorsExternal = an independent evaluator providing objective attestation, industry benchmarks and regulatory assurance (Q84, Q104); internal = process knowledge, ongoing improvement — but employer conflict remains. Management owns controls; auditors assure. Continuous monitoring supplements rather than replaces periodic attestation (Q115, Q139).
Audit toolingCOBIT = governance/business alignment · ITAF = audit methodology · NIST SP 800-53A = control assessment. Predictive analytics = proactive anticipation of fraud, control degradation, risk hotspots (Q74, Q122). Emerging-tech audits align to risk strategy first, adapt evidence (API-based, cloud-native, SMEs) (Q92); core skills challenge = keeping pace with evolution (Q131). Integrated cross-domain findings need recurring collaboration meetings across IT, security, legal, compliance, HR and business units — periodic report sharing is passive and one-way (Q137).
Emerging techDistributed, ephemeral, immutable or safety-critical environments: runtime and continuous tests beat static images, diagrams and quarterly snapshots (6.3 §5). IoT: heterogeneity of devices/OS/firmware/protocols defeats uniform tools — weak defaults and limited compute break agent-based scanning (Q68, Q87, Q114) · ICS/SCADA: safety + availability outrank depth — least intrusive adequate test (Q126) · Edge: distributed, physically exposed nodes, intermittent connectivity; dynamically test edge-to-cloud flows (MQTT/CoAP/AMQP) — static firmware misses cross-component behavior (Q1, Q132) · Containers: ephemeral → runtime assessment; shared host kernel — privileged mode and mounted Docker sockets raise escape risk; escape = top blast radius (Q121, Q136) · Blockchain: immutable → pre-deploy verification (Q101) · AR/VR: spatial/sensor/environmental-capture manipulation and privacy (Q59) · Mobile: permissions, sandbox, APIs — web-only is incomplete (Q112) · Zero trust: prove enforcement by attempted east-west movement and identity-based decisions, not diagrams (Q7); in a microsegmented network, discovering and reaching all isolated segments is the foundational challenge — persistence is irrelevant before coverage (Q123) · Multi-cloud: CSPM + IAM policy + API testing + provider-boundary review (Q78).
ResilienceRTO = maximum acceptable restore time; RPO = maximum acceptable data-loss window; criticality + dependencies set recovery order. Exam wording: testing results can help identify critical systems and prioritize recovery; in real planning the BIA, dependencies, criticality, RTO/RPO are authoritative — testing validates or corrects (Q45). DR test ladder: checklist → structured walk-through → simulation → parallel → full interruption (management-authorized; fidelity and disruption rise together).
Process evidenceAccount reviews expose privilege creep, dormant/orphaned accounts; awareness = behavioral metrics (phishing clicks, reporting) — completion is activity (Q107); backup verification proves data written, only a successful restore test proves recoverability; ISCM = near-real-time awareness via automated high-frequency assessment (Q140); PDCA closes the loop; a logged finding is not closure — owner, remediation, verification, reassessment.

Cues that point here

  • Customer due-diligence detail → SOC 2 Type II; public page → SOC 3
  • Operating effectiveness over time → Type II (Q48, Q96)
  • Unbiased IR evaluation + peer benchmarks → external auditor (Q104)
  • Maximum DR realism → full interruption
  • Prove backups → restore test

Traps

  • Type II “gold standard” ≠ zero risk and does not outrank every framework for every purpose.
  • SOC 2 is an attestation, not a certification; ISO 27001 ≠ control-test detail.
  • Never assume all five TSC are in scope — only Security is mandatory.
  • Auditors do not own or implement controls — management does.

8 · Trap strip

The sixteen discriminating facts that flip the most answers.

  1. SCAP standardizes content — it is not a scanner. Adopting SCAP-compliant tools solves interoperability, not discovery (Q24).
  2. An unauthenticated scan with zero findings does not prove security — shallow visibility produces false negatives.
  3. False negatives are worse than false positives: real flaw, unreported, false confidence (Q19).
  4. External ≠ independent. A second-party customer assessment is external yet conflicted.
  5. BAS emulates attacks — it never assigns CVE identifiers (Q43).
  6. Assumed breach starts inside — little evidence about perimeter defense (Q69).
  7. Purple is a process, not a permanent third squad; its metric is persistent detection/response improvement (Q75).
  8. Off-peak destructive testing still risks disruption — RoE and non-destructive methods decide (Q73).
  9. Automated tools miss business logic — complex workflow abuse needs human review (Q81).
  10. Pseudonymized test data ≠ anonymimized — reversible identifiers keep residual privacy risk (Q128).
  11. Circular logging loses the earliest forensic evidence — overwritten, not corrupted (Q18).
  12. Forwarding noisy events to the SIEM moves the problem — clip or correlate at source (Q17).
  13. Test counts are activity; multi-year critical time-to-patch is effectiveness (Q23).
  14. Training completion is activity — measure phishing clicks and reporting behavior (Q107).
  15. A successful backup job ≠ recoverability — only a successful restore test proves it.
  16. A favorable SOC report is not a guarantee — read scope, period, exceptions, CUECs, residual risk.
