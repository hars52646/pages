Cues that point here

  • “Most volatile” with registers/cache offered → registers/cache; without them → RAM (Q61 Q67 Q213).
  • Hash match proves integrity; custody documentation proves handling — different questions (Q3 Q117).
  • Suspected encryption or rootkit → memory acquisition before shutdown (Q79 Q208).
  • Scenario conclusions: preserve-then-analyze wins over reimage-first (Q60 Q80 Q82 Q85 Q86); custody gaps surface as integrity challenges (Q87 Q117); multi-source timeline correlation beats single-source review (Q149 Q163 Q174 Q176).
  • Five qualities, verbatim cues: admissible = legally accepted in the proceeding; authentic = attributable to the origin it claims; complete = tells the whole story fairly, never selectively or misleadingly; reliable = trustworthy and withstands scrutiny; believability = the fact-finder can believe it — custody prevents substituted, altered or contaminated evidence (Q116 Q218).
  • Workflow identify step: identification recognizes and categorizes potentially relevant evidence (Q1 Q83).
  • A backup is not a forensic image: do not treat an ordinary backup as forensically equivalent — it is not necessarily acquired or hashed that way (Q3 Q83).
  • Never analyze the original: no antivirus scans, ordinary copying or swap-out/visual inspection — they can alter or omit content and structural data (Q120 Q157).
  • Locard is bidirectional: the intruder leaves artifacts AND takes information — data exfiltration means every intrusion is an exchange of evidence (Q84 Q206).
  • Why logging pays: attacker interactions captured in logs become valuable evidence — the forensic justification, not performance tuning (Q83).
  • Classification anchors: real evidence = the tangible object (laptop, SSD, USB drive), stored and classified separately; hearsay = reporting what a colleague allegedly said, not firsthand observation; secondary = a reproduction, even though the printout is tangible paper; corroborative = independently supports another source, not merely retelling it (Q127 Q147 Q151).
  • Printout trap: tangibility does not convert a reproduction into original evidence (Q151).
  • Cloud detail: correlate across major multiple providers while preserving provenance; containers disappear — transient instances rapidly created, moved and scaled; centralized evidence management promotes consistent identifiers with provenance intact; client-key encryption is a greater obstacle than timestomping, metadata manipulation or compression (Q32 Q44 Q50 Q184).
  • Cross-border: law outranks logistics — conflicting laws beat time-zone coordination, language translation and incompatible tooling (Q164 Q214).
  • GDPR pair: transferring unencrypted EU data to US servers lacks lawful basis and safeguards; the 72-hour breach notification is a duty, not the concern (Q74).
  • AI-only rejected: end-to-end AI without independent validation weakens reliability and defensibility (Q227).
  • Social media: capture rapidly changing content with specialized tools that preserve metadata in a forensically sound manner; user-provided screenshots weakly omit metadata and provenance (Q219).
  • Scenario conclusions: unusual traces demonstrate the attacker inevitably leaves digital evidence (Q60); complete elimination is practically impossible — interactions cannot all be erased (Q82); collection has side effects — preserve format, mind throughput (Q174).

Traps

  • Reimaging to restore service before imaging = evidence destroyed (Q60 Q226).
  • A backup is not a forensic image; verification hash is not custody (Q3 Q117).
  • Social media / IoT evidence: capture with timestamps + legal basis (Q184 Q219 Q245).

g6 · Business Continuity & BIA

people first · BIA · criticality — 7.3/7.4.csv · destcert BCM mindmap

Continuity exists to keep people safe, then minimize damage, then assure business survival. The BIA is the first analytical step: identify critical functions through their dependencies, quantify impact over time, and let tolerances (RTO/RPO/MTD) drive every recovery decision downstream.

  • Direct resources toward the functions that threaten organizational survival — never equally across departments; equal allocation wastes scarce resources on non-essential functions (the fairness trap) (Q128 Q153).
  • Payroll can be critical: essential for workforce continuity — omitting it while customer-facing systems are restored is the classic violation (Q110).
  • Compliance can be critical: accounting and regulatory reporting carry penalties and missed filing deadlines (Q126).

  • Spill scenario: evacuate before attempting any chemical containment (Q150).
  • Sequence violation: damage containment completed before evacuation finishes — the step that came too soon (Q162).

  • Executive support is necessary but the method the exam scores is BIA-first sequencing (Q7 Q197).

g7 · Disaster Recovery & Resilience

RTO/RPO · sites · RAID — 7.3/7.4.csv · destcert recovery-strategies mindmap

Recovery is arithmetic plus choreography: BIA tolerances (RPO, RTO+WRT ≤ MTD) select the strategy; declaration follows pre-agreed criteria with a named authority; restoration runs dependencies first; failback runs non-critical first; and resilience engineering (RAID, HA, backups, spares, suppliers) keeps the whole ladder affordable.

Hash ≠ chain of custody. A matching hash proves integrity of content; custody documentation proves handling. Gaps challenge integrity — they do not auto-exclude.Q3 · Q117
Backup ≠ forensic image. Backups skip deleted/slack space; only a write-blocked bit-for-bit image with hashes is forensic.Q3 · Q83 · Q120
Never analyze the original. Image first, hash, library copy, then work on disposable copies.Q1 · Q120 · Q227
Most volatile offered wins. Registers/cache first; if not offered, RAM — not disk or logs.Q61 · Q67 · Q213
Printed logs are secondary evidence. Verify against the source system before relying on them.Q151
Hourly signature updates still miss unknown malware. Signature methods only match catalogued patterns.Q109 · Q14
FIM proves change, not identity. It answers “did it change?”, never “which malware?”.Q160
Monitoring is detective. Asked to prevent zero-day impact, choose preventive compensating controls.Q211
Retention — not media age — authorizes disposal.Q103 · Q233
Same-location duplicates keep the SPOF. Redundant media must be geographically separated.Q106
MTD = RTO + WRT arithmetic. 6h + 4h > 8h MTD fails the requirement — compute before choosing.Q132 · Q115
RPO looks backward to the last backup, not forward to the next one.Q52 · Q114 · Q130
Don’t zero out WRT to save RTO. Data verification time is real and non-negotiable.Q133
